# Setting up the company (Nida as sole owner)

General information to get started, not legal or tax advice. Confirm the choices with an accountant or lawyer before filing. Fees are approximate and change; check the official sites.

## 1. Decide two things first
- **Federal or Ontario.**
  - **Federal** (Corporations Canada, online, about $200): the name is protected across Canada. Then register the corporation in Ontario too (Ontario Business Registry), because that's where it operates.
  - **Ontario** (Ontario Business Registry, online, about $300): simpler if it will only ever operate in Ontario.
  - A common choice for a consumer brand with a distinctive name is **federal**, for the national name protection. Ask the accountant.
- **The name.** Order a **NUANS name search** for "The 6 Pass" (a few dollars to tens of dollars online) to check it isn't too close to an existing business name. Note that "6ixpass"-style names exist; the trademark search (step 4) matters too.

## 2. Incorporate
- **Do it yourself** on the government site, or use a service such as Ownr (a few hundred dollars) that fills in the forms and minute book. A lawyer typically costs more but handles everything.
- Nida is the **only director and only shareholder**. Keep the minute book (articles, bylaws, share register, first resolutions) somewhere safe.
- If Ammar may get shares later, ask the lawyer how to leave room for that now.

## 3. Right after incorporating
- **Business number (BN):** issued by the CRA as part of incorporation.
- **HST:** registration is required once revenue passes $30,000 in four quarters; registering earlier can let the business claim HST it pays on costs. Ask the accountant, especially about how HST applies to the pass price.
- **Business bank account** in the company's name. Keep business and personal money separate from day one.
- **Insurance:** commercial general liability, and ask about cyber/privacy cover once there's a member database.
- **Email and domain:** make sure the6pass.ca and hello@the6pass.ca are owned by the company's account.

## 4. Protect the brand
- **Trademark search** for "The 6 Pass" in the Canadian Trademarks Database (CIPO), free to search. Consider filing an application (government fees apply; a trademark agent can help).

## 5. Lawyer, for the partner side
- **Partner agreement:** turns signed letters of intent into Founding Partners. Should cover: the 12 months free, no commission, no setup, 30 days' notice, offer rules (food and non-alcoholic only; at least three days a week; uses per member; blackout dates; pause with 7 days' notice), use of the partner's name and photos, and what happens if either side ends it.
- Later, before members pay: final **terms of use**, **privacy policy**, refund policy, and the Ontario consumer protection rules for memberships and auto-renewal.
